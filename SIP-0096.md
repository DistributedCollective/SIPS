# SIP-0096: Perimeter Withdrawal Delay

## Summary

SIP-0096 completes the withdrawal protection introduced as phase 2 of the Perimeter programme in [SIP-0094](SIP-0094.md). It adds a configurable delay to covered user withdrawals so suspicious exits can be reviewed before funds leave the protocol. Covered withdrawals are held in an exit delay queue and can be claimed by their recorded recipient after the delay. The queue also supports pausing claims, blocking addresses, and bounded recovery of funds in confirmed incidents.

The proposal updates the lending and Zero withdrawal paths and adds the queue and its controller logic. It is submitted in two GovernorOwner proposals to fit the governor's ten-action limit: Part 1 has nine actions for lending; Part 2 has five actions for Zero, or four if no CollSurplusPool upgrade is needed. The delay remains off until installation and activation gates pass.

## Motivation

Without a delay, a withdrawal can leave the protocol in the same transaction in which an exploit begins. Escrowing covered exits creates time to identify and respond to an attack. Users will need to initiate a withdrawal and claim it after its unlock time, adding a delay and a second transaction to the withdrawal experience.

## Proposed changes

### Part 1 — Lending

Register `LoanTokenLogicLM` and `LoanTokenLogicWrbtcLM` on their two beacons; install `LoanClosingsWith`, `LoanClosingsWithSwap`, the current `LoanMaintenance` and `LoanMaintenanceViews`, and `ExitFeeModule`; then set the borrower settlement companion and queue pointers. These nine actions apply the configured delay to covered user exits, including iToken redemptions, borrower collateral withdrawals, and loan-close payouts.

The `FeeSharingCollector` upgrade is separate from Part 1. Before Part 1 executes, apply and read back the Collector's paired zero-fee and delay-bypass exemptions. After Part 1 installs the two-return iWRBTC redemption module, the Exchequer multisig upgrades the existing Collector proxy with the no-argument implementation. The proxy's existing protocol and staking pointers remain intact; it is not initialized again. The SIP-0094 fee stays enabled, and the new delay stays off until installation and activation gates pass.

Forced settlement paths such as liquidation and rollover remain direct and are not delayed. Read-only queries, collateral deposits, and maintenance actions that do not pay out a covered user exit are unchanged.

### Part 2 — Zero

Update the Zero borrower withdrawal and collateral-surplus paths to apply the same delay and queue behavior. Update `BorrowerOperations`, `BorrowerOperationsPerimeterOps`, `CollSurplusPool`, and `TroveManager`, including the Recovery Mode correction, and connect borrower exits to the queue. Redemptions and other forced settlement paths remain direct and are not delayed.

### Queue and controller behavior

For a covered exit while the delay is enabled, the net user payout is recorded in the queue with a fixed recipient and unlock time. After unlock, the recorded eligible parties can claim it. Anyone may deliver an unlocked, unblocked contract-owned request, but payment can go only to its immutable recorded receiver; the caller cannot redirect it. This is ordinary delivery, not guardian resolution. Authorized incident controls can pause claims or block addresses; recovery is limited to the queue's configured routes and request conditions. Disabling the delay stops new holds but does not alter withdrawals already queued. The delay control is independent of SIP-0094's fee control.

The delay applies only to configured exit surfaces. Fees continue to follow SIP-0094. This proposal does not authorize a treasury transfer.

### Frontend

Update the Sovryn dapp to show the delay and unlock time on covered withdrawals, and to let users view and claim their queued withdrawals after unlock.
